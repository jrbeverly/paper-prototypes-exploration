---
title: Paper Prototypes
---

Much of the conversation around AI-assisted development focuses on writing better
specifications. The assumption is that more detailed documents produce better
software.

**That assumption is worth questioning.**

Specifications communicate understanding. They do not create it. When teams write
comprehensive specs before they understand the problem, they produce documents
that look complete while encoding wrong assumptions.

The faster path to better software is to build understanding first. Cheap,
disposable prototypes are the most effective tool for doing that.

---

## Prototypes are questions

Every prototype should answer a question. *Would this workflow make sense? Is
this interaction intuitive? Does this architecture feel maintainable?*

A prototype is not an early implementation. It is an experiment. Build it,
observe what you learn, and discard it. The cost of the prototype is the price
of the answer.

AI makes this dramatically cheaper. Instead of debating whether an idea might
work, teams can build an example in hours or minutes and evaluate it directly.

**AI should increase the frequency of experimentation, not the length of
specifications.**

---

## Write the spec after you understand

Specifications remain valuable. But they are most valuable when they capture
understanding that has already been validated.

The recommended sequence: identify a problem, build a prototype, observe
behaviour, refine your understanding, and only then document the conclusions.

The resulting specification is grounded in evidence rather than speculation.

---

## Who this is for

This project speaks to anyone who wants better outcomes from software design:
engineers, architects, technical leads, engineering managers, product managers,
and AI-assisted developers.

If you have ever written a detailed specification only to discover during
implementation that the design needed to change, the ideas here are for you.
